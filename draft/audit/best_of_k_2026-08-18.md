# BEST-OF-K — the null `BLEND-SEARCH-DESIGN` says it needs most, built and calibrated

_TERRITORY: D. `BLEND-SEARCH-DESIGN.md` §3 step 2, due **09-10**, owner relay.
Written 2026-08-18. It existed nowhere in the repo — `grep` for
`best_of_k|BEST_OF_K` returned zero hits._

> *"BEST-OF-K is the one this program does not yet have and needs most. With
> five arms today it barely matters; with fifteen arms and blends it is the
> difference between a finding and a coin flip."*

Built as a **reusable tool**, the shape that worked for `collinearity_check.py`:
one call, before you believe a winner.

## 1. WHAT IT MEASURES

Given K arms scored on the **same rows**, permute the arm labels within each
row — preserving how hard each row was, destroying arm skill — and recompute
every arm's MAE. That gives the distribution of winning margins from K arms
that are all equally good.

## 2. THE CALIBRATION — free margin, per unit of per-row error sd

**Multiply by your study's own error sd to get the free margin in points.**

| K | n=200 | n=500 | n=1000 | n=2000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.0388 | 0.0247 | 0.0179 | 0.0136 |
| 5 | 0.0815 | 0.0533 | 0.0378 | 0.0266 |
| 10 | 0.1088 | 0.0690 | 0.0485 | 0.0349 |
| **20** | **0.1337** | **0.0827** | **0.0588** | **0.0425** |

**On 500 rows, the best of 5 no-skill arms beats the field by 0.053 × sd for
free; the best of 20 by 0.083 — 1.6× more.** That is the design doc's warning
turned into a number a study can be sized against **before** it runs.

## 3. I GOT THE STATISTIC WRONG FIRST, AND THE CALIBRATION TABLE CAUGHT IT

The first version used **margin over the runner-up**. That quantity **shrinks**
as K grows — add arms and the top two bunch together:

| K | runner-up margin, n=500 |
|---|---|
| 2 | 0.0493 |
| 20 | 0.0203 |

**It is a valid test and it is the opposite of the risk the design doc
describes.** The statistic that grows with K is the margin over the **field
mean**, and that is now the headline. The runner-up gap still ships as
secondary, because a champion barely ahead of second place is a different
worry.

**I found this by printing the calibration table before shipping**, which is
the only reason it did not go out backwards. A test now pins both directions.

## 4. AND THE LIMITATION, FOUND BY RUNNING IT ON MY OWN WORK

`opponent_arm.py` picked a λ per position from an 8-point grid — that is a
best-of-K selection with K=8, so the tool's first application was to my own
choice:

| position | n | winner | field margin | null | p | survives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 1,075 | λ=0.15 | +0.0945 | +0.0499 | 0.0066 | ✅ |
| RB | 1,690 | λ=0.35 | +0.0210 | +0.0264 | 0.701 | ❌ |
| WR | 2,494 | λ=0.15 | +0.0291 | +0.0156 | 0.030 | ✅ |
| TE | 872 | **λ=0.00** | +0.0828 | +0.0335 | 0.0033 | ✅ |

**TE's surviving "winner" is λ = 0.00 — do nothing.** That is true, and it says
only that the tilts hurt TE.

> **best-of-K compares the winner to THE FIELD, not to doing nothing. If the
> field contains arms already known to be bad, the field margin is inflated and
> the p-value flatters the winner.**

A parameter sweep including λ=1.00 is not a field of serious candidates. **The
fifteen-arm future the design doc plans for is exactly where a few weak arms
would inflate the field**, so the caveat is now in the module's own docstring
rather than only here.

**RB failing is the honest one:** its λ choice does *not* beat what a no-skill
panel would produce, which is consistent with the arm's out-of-sample verdict
(2 of 3 seasons, register DS8).

## 5. THE CONTROLS ARE THE POINT

A null that cannot fire is worse than no null, because it launders every future
winner.

| control | result |
|---|---|
| **known-positive** — one arm made genuinely better | detected, p<0.05 |
| **known-negative** — 5 arms of identical skill, 12 seeds | fired **0–2 of 12**, within the advertised rate |
| **direction** — field margin must grow with K | pinned, both tables |
| **different row counts** | raises, rather than silently comparing populations |
| **sd scaling** | linear, so the published per-sd table can be multiplied |

## 6. WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO

- **It does not replace a baseline comparison.** §4 is the whole reason: include
  an explicit do-nothing arm and read it directly.
- **It does not handle blends** — a stacker fitted on the same rows it is scored
  on needs the RANDOM-WEIGHT null, which is a different tool and still owed.
- **It assumes arms are scored on identical rows**, and refuses otherwise.
- **Not wired into the Tuesday grader.** That is step 2's other half and is
  A's/relay's call; the tool is importable today.
